What the replay actually found

Not the answer I expected when I built this, and the most useful number in the case study.

0.000 t from nesting the plate more tightly. Software uses 380 mm less plate height than the hand nest and recovers exactly nothing, because the leftover was never measured.
0.172 t from measuring the same leftover and recording where it is. Same nest, same steel, same parts. Only the record changes.

Your nesting team said automatic nesting wastes more than doing it by hand. On this plate they are right, narrowly: the hand nest consumes 380 mm more plate height. But that advantage never reaches the ledger, because the material it saves is handed back as an offcut nobody can find. This is what you told us: "I don't think they use the offcuts as well as they could be. The idea is already there, but it could be done better."

Settles the meeting

Your three wastage numbers agree with each other.

Plate 20 to 25%, profiles 5 to 10%, overall 7 to 10%. I tested whether those three can all be true at once. They can, for any split up to about a third of your tonnage being plate. So there is no correction to make to your figures. What is missing is the basis behind them.

Green strip is your stated 7 to 10%. Orange lines are everything your own plate and profile rates allow at each plate share. They overlap until 33.3%, which is why the two ranges are not in conflict. An early draft of this analysis tested the wrong edge of the band, found no solution, and nearly told you your own numbers were wrong.

THE TEST THAT FAILS

Wrong edge

Asks whether the band's upper bound falls below 10%. It never does, so the test returns "impossible". Correct answer, wrong question.

THE TEST THAT WORKS

Overlap

Asks whether the lower bound clears the top of your band: 5% + 15P ≤ 10%, so P ≤ 33.3%. Held for every P at or below a third.

THE COUNTER-EXAMPLE

P = 15%

0.15(20%) + 0.85(5%) = 7.25%

Inside your 7 to 10%. One number, settles it in a meeting.

Honesty

What this prototype is not.

Every number above is computed in your browser by a deterministic solver from analysis/packer.js. Nothing is drawn by hand and nothing is asserted.

1

The plate geometry is synthetic. 15 invented structural parts on a 3000 × 2000 × 12 mm plate. It is not your steel, your thicknesses, or your parts. It proves the shape of the argument, not your yield.

2

It is not a nesting optimiser. A first-fit-decreasing shelf packer is roughly the dumbest thing that works. A real solver would beat every arm here, including the Advisor.

3

There is no model in it. No AI, no learned weights. If you needed AI to get this result, you would not have trusted it, and I would not be showing it to you in week one.

4

One plate is not a baseline. The replay runs on at least 40 nests across 5 closed projects. A single plate is a bug report, not a business case.

5

But the arithmetic is real, and it is checkable. node analysis/packer.js reproduces the table above and prints every finding. node tests/test_packer.js asserts all 13 of them, 13/13 passing.

The decision

Buy nothing until week 4.

The Advisor is only worth building if it beats a hand nest once you have measured one. That measurement is four weeks of replay on your own closed projects, cuts no steel, and needs no change to anyone's routine. If the hand nests win, that is a real answer and you keep it.
